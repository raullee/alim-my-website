Known gap

What's not in here yet

Entertainment licensing, public order by-laws and Syariah criminal offences sit on the State List, so the rules that decide whether a show can happen differ in every state and are not published on the federal portal at all. Neither are most local authority by-laws.

That gap is the single biggest one in any Malaysian legal resource for the arts, this one included. Building it out state by state is a standing ALIM research task, and the pages here say so rather than pretend otherwise.

Method

How this library is kept honest

Every Act number, title and consolidation date is read from the AGC catalogue, not from memory. Where AGC publishes a consolidated “as at” text, that is the copy shown here, not the original enactment.

Section numbers under What to read first are extracted from the PDF itself. Where a number could not be confirmed, the entry is labelled topic rather than carrying a number we guessed. Where AGC publishes only a scan with no text layer, the page says so instead of showing an empty list.

This page is published by ALIM (Art × Law Initiative Malaysia) as general legal information for Malaysia’s creative communities. It is not legal advice. The law changes. Each Act reproduced here is the Attorney General’s Chambers’ consolidated reprint and carries the date it was consolidated to; amendments made after that date do not appear in the text, and the authoritative version of any Malaysian law is the one published in the Federal Gazette. Subsidiary legislation, state enactments and local authority by-laws may also apply to your situation and are largely outside this library.
