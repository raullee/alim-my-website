ALIM legislation library — scenario claims for counsel review
Each claim is one step of one scenario as it currently appears on alim.my.
The hosted PDF for each Act is at legislation/pdf/<id>-bi.pdf and is the AGC
consolidated reprint 'as at' the date shown. Amendments after that date are NOT
in the hosted text, so check them separately.

SCENARIO dj-club-night  |  DJ a club night
LEDE: You are playing other people's recordings to a paying room. Three separate licences, one venue licence, a state layer, and a tax nobody budgets for.
WATCH-CLAIM: The single most common failure is the room assuming one licence covers the music. It does not. MACP, PRISM and RPM are three separate rights held by three separate bodies.

[dj-club-night#01] Collecting societies (collecting)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmos-bi.pdf
    ROLES: venue, promoter
    CLAIM: Playing recorded music to a paying room needs public performance licences from more than one body: MACP for the composition, PRISM for the performers, RPM for the recording. A room holding one of the three is under-licensed, and the liability lands on whoever put the night on.

[dj-club-night#02] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: dj, promoter, venue
    CLAIM: Public performance and communication to the public are acts the copyright owner controls. Your own edits, bootlegs and mashups are derivative works: making one is a permission, playing it is a second, releasing it is a third.

[dj-club-night#03] Entertainment (Federal Territory of Kuala Lumpur) Act 1992 (Act 493)  as-at 2013-02-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-493-bi.pdf
    ROLES: venue, promoter
    CLAIM: In Kuala Lumpur the premises needs an entertainment licence, and the conditions attached to it are where hours and content are actually controlled. The licence, not a statute, is usually what a shutdown is based on.

[dj-club-night#04] State entertainment enactments (state_enactment)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/state-entertainment-bi.pdf
    ROLES: venue, promoter
    CLAIM: Outside KL the same power sits in state enactments and council by-laws, and they differ by state. Confirm the licensing position before you confirm the date.

[dj-club-night#05] Local Government Act 1976 (Act 171)  as-at 2012-12-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-171-bi.pdf
    ROLES: venue, promoter
    CLAIM: Operating hours, amplified noise and premises licensing come from local authority by-laws made under this Act. If you are being moved on or closed early, this is usually why.

[dj-club-night#06] Entertainments Duty Act 1953 (Act 103)  as-at 2023-12-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-103-bi.pdf
    ROLES: promoter
    CLAIM: Entertainments duty is charged on paid admissions and administered by the state. It is a real line on a ticketed show's budget and first-time promoters price the ticket without it.

[dj-club-night#07] Income Tax Act 1967 (Act 53)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-53-bi.pdf
    ROLES: promoter
    CLAIM: Book a non-resident DJ and s.109A withholding on payments to non-resident public entertainers is your liability as the payer, not the artist's. Failing to deduct makes you personally liable for the tax plus a 10 per cent increase.

[dj-club-night#08] Immigration Act 1959/63 (Act 155)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-155-bi.pdf
    ROLES: promoter
    CLAIM: A performance is work, so a foreign DJ needs the right pass. A social visit pass is the wrong pass and the exposure runs to the person who brought them in.

[dj-club-night#09] PUSPAL (guideline)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/puspal-bi.pdf
    ROLES: promoter
    CLAIM: Foreign performers also need PUSPAL clearance before the immigration pass issues, and that is usually the binding constraint on your timeline, not the pass itself.

[dj-club-night#10] Contracts Act 1950 (Act 136)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-136-bi.pdf
    ROLES: dj, promoter, venue
    CLAIM: The booking is a contract whether or not anyone signed anything. Section 28 makes agreements in restraint of trade void, which is why most radius and exclusivity clauses in Malaysian booking agreements are weaker than they read.

[dj-club-night#11] Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (Act 514)  as-at 2024-06-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-514-bi.pdf
    ROLES: venue, promoter
    CLAIM: A venue and a rig are a workplace. The 2022 amendments reach the principal, so a promoter cannot subcontract the safety risk down to the crew's own employer.

[dj-club-night#12] Anti-Sexual Harassment Act 2022 (Act 840)  as-at 2022-10-18  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-840-bi.pdf
    ROLES: dj, promoter, venue
    CLAIM: The Tribunal takes complaints from outside an employment relationship, which is exactly the shape harm takes in nightlife: a freelancer and a gatekeeper who is not their employer.

[dj-club-night#13] Penal Code (Act 574)  as-at 2023-07-04  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-574-bi.pdf
    ROLES: dj
    CLAIM: Section 294 reaches an obscene song, ballad or words in or near a public place. It is the provision behind most complaints about what came out of the booth or the mic.

[dj-club-night#14] Service Tax Act 2018 (Act 807)  as-at 2025-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-807-bi.pdf
    ROLES: promoter, venue
    CLAIM: Once past the registration threshold, event and related services can be taxable. The list is widened by order most years, so check the current schedule rather than a remembered figure.

SCENARIO post-set-online  |  Post a set, reel or performance video
LEDE: Uploading is reproduction and communication to the public, and then four separate speech regimes sit on top of it.
WATCH-CLAIM: Removal and prosecution are different problems with different cures. Most artists prepare for the charge that rarely comes and have no plan for the takedown that does.

[post-set-online#01] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator, organiser
    CLAIM: A recorded set or a reel with music in it contains other people's recordings and compositions. Uploading is both reproduction and communication to the public, which is why content matching pulls sets down even when nobody complained.

[post-set-online#02] Collecting societies (collecting)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmos-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator, organiser
    CLAIM: A licence the venue holds for the room does not travel with the video. Platform-level licensing covers some uses and not others, and never covers a full recorded set.

[post-set-online#03] Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (Act 588)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-588-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator
    CLAIM: Section 233 is the provision most often used against people who post: offensive, indecent, obscene or menacing content, and content sent with intent to annoy. Penalties were raised in 2024. This is the MCMC route.

[post-set-online#04] Content Code (industry_code)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmcf-content-code-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator
    CLAIM: Registered under s.213 of the Communications and Multimedia Act, so compliance is a defence. Day to day it does more work than the Act: it is the standard platforms, broadcasters and advertisers actually apply when they decide whether your work goes out.

[post-set-online#05] Online Safety Act 2025 (Act 866)  as-at 2025-05-22  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-866-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator, subject
    CLAIM: In force from 1 January 2026. Your exposure here is not a charge, it is silent removal by a platform acting on a statutory duty, and a removal is far harder to appeal than a charge is to defend.

[post-set-online#06] Film Censorship Act 2002 (Act 620)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-620-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator
    CLAIM: If what you posted is a film, Board approval is the gate, and the Board reads film widely enough to catch shorts, music videos and festival material.

[post-set-online#07] Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709)  as-at 2023-07-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-709-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator, organiser
    CLAIM: Filming the room means processing other people's personal data. Breach notification and the data protection officer duty arrived with the 2024 amendments and are being enforced.

[post-set-online#08] Defamation Act 1957 (Act 286)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-286-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator, subject
    CLAIM: What you say over the top, or in the caption, is publication. It also cuts the other way: this is your statute if someone destroys your reputation in a post.

[post-set-online#09] Federal Constitution (constitution)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/fedcon-bi.pdf
    ROLES: creator
    CLAIM: Article 10(1)(a) is the right every statute above restricts, and Article 10(2)(a) is the power they are made under. Every challenge to a takedown or a charge starts here.

SCENARIO busking  |  Busk or perform in the street
LEDE: Almost none of the rules that decide this are federal, and none of the ones that matter are on the government's legislation portal.
WATCH-CLAIM: This is the clearest example of the gap in every Malaysian legal resource for the arts: the operative rule is a council by-law nobody publishes centrally.

[busking#01] Local Government Act 1976 (Act 171)  as-at 2012-12-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-171-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer, organiser
    CLAIM: The permit is a by-law made by the local council under this Act. DBKL, MBPJ and every other local authority licence busking separately and publish separately, so there is no national answer and none of it is on the federal portal.

[busking#02] Minor Offences Act 1955 (Act 336)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-336-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer
    CLAIM: Begging and public nuisance offences, read with those by-laws, are how street performance is actually policed. Whether you are performing or soliciting alms is the whole question and it gets decided on the street, not in a court.

[busking#03] Penal Code (Act 574)  as-at 2023-07-04  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-574-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer
    CLAIM: Section 294 reaches an obscene song, ballad or words in or near a public place.

[busking#04] Collecting societies (collecting)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmos-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer
    CLAIM: Performing other people's songs in public is a public performance. Enforcement against buskers is rare, but the right is real and organised street programmes get asked.

[busking#05] Peaceful Assembly Act 2012 (Act 736)  as-at 2019-12-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-736-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer, organiser
    CLAIM: A crowd that forms around you can be characterised as an assembly, and the notification requirement in s.9(1) survived the 2021 challenge even though s.9(5) did not.

[busking#06] State entertainment enactments (state_enactment)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/state-entertainment-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer, organiser
    CLAIM: Some states require an entertainment licence even for unamplified street performance.

[busking#07] Federal Constitution (constitution)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/fedcon-bi.pdf
    ROLES: performer
    CLAIM: Article 10 is the backstop when a by-law written for hawkers is turned on expression.

SCENARIO release-track  |  Release and distribute a track
LEDE: Ownership, registration and the two separate rights in every recording.
WATCH-CLAIM: Most Malaysian artists are members of nothing. The recording can be everywhere and the public performance money stays unclaimed because no one registered the work.

[release-track#01] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: Two rights live in every release: the composition and the sound recording, often owned by different people. Section 26 gives first ownership, s.26(2) quietly moves it for commissioned work and work made in employment, and s.27 requires assignments and exclusive licences to be in writing. Malaysia has no copyright registration: protection is automatic on fixation.

[release-track#02] Collecting societies (collecting)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmos-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: Register with MACP as a writer and PRISM as a performer or you collect nothing from public performance and broadcast no matter how heavily you are played.

[release-track#03] Trademarks Act 2019 (Act 815)  as-at 2022-03-18  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-815-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: The artist name and the label name. Registration gives a monopoly in the registered classes; without it you are left with passing off, which is slower and harder to prove.

[release-track#04] Berne Convention (treaty)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/berne-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: You are protected in every member state automatically, with no registration or formality. That is the answer to whether you need to register your copyright overseas.

[release-track#05] WPPT (treaty)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/wppt-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: The international floor for performers' and producers' rights, and the source of the performers' rights provisions in the Copyright Act.

[release-track#06] Contracts Act 1950 (Act 136)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-136-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: Splits, producer points, the distribution deal and the label agreement are all contract. Section 28 makes restraint of trade void, which weakens most exclusivity.

[release-track#07] Stamp Act 1949 (Act 378)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-378-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: An assignment of copyright is an instrument. Unstamped, it can be shut out of evidence at the exact moment you need to prove who owns the record.

[release-track#08] Income Tax Act 1967 (Act 53)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-53-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, label
    CLAIM: Royalty income is income, and withholding applies to royalties paid out of Malaysia to non-residents.

[release-track#09] Optical Discs Act 2000 (Act 606)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-606-bi.pdf
    ROLES: label
    CLAIM: Still the live instrument for a physical pressing run.

SCENARIO sync-licence  |  Licence a song for an ad, film or game
LEDE: Two permissions, usually from two different owners, and a blanket licence is neither of them.
WATCH-CLAIM: The two-rights problem is where almost every Malaysian sync falls over: a brand clears the recording from a label and never clears the song from the writers.

[sync-licence#01] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, buyer
    CLAIM: A sync needs two clearances: the composition and the sound recording. They are separate rights and frequently separate owners, and clearing one is worth nothing without the other. Section 27 requires an exclusive licence to be in writing.

[sync-licence#02] Collecting societies (collecting)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmos-bi.pdf
    ROLES: buyer
    CLAIM: A blanket public performance licence from MACP, PRISM or RPM is not a sync licence and never has been. Sync is negotiated directly with the owners.

[sync-licence#03] Contracts Act 1950 (Act 136)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-136-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, buyer
    CLAIM: Term, territory, media, exclusivity and the most-negotiated clause of all: what happens on renewal.

[sync-licence#04] Stamp Act 1949 (Act 378)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-378-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, buyer
    CLAIM: Stamp it. An unstamped licence is inadmissible until duty and penalty are paid.

[sync-licence#05] Income Tax Act 1967 (Act 53)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-53-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, buyer
    CLAIM: Withholding applies to royalties paid out of Malaysia to non-residents, and the payer carries the obligation.

[sync-licence#06] Trademarks Act 2019 (Act 815)  as-at 2022-03-18  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-815-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: Use of your artist name in the credit or the campaign is a separate question from use of the music.

[sync-licence#07] Berne Convention (treaty)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/berne-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: National treatment means a Malaysian work is protected in the buyer's territory without formality.

SCENARIO documentary  |  Shoot and screen a film or documentary
LEDE: Approval is the gate, a licence is the barrier to entry, and possession alone is an offence.
WATCH-CLAIM: Section 6 of the Film Censorship Act catches possession. A finished unapproved cut sitting on your drive is already inside the offence.

[documentary#01] Film Censorship Act 2002 (Act 620)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-620-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker, exhibitor
    CLAIM: No film may be distributed, exhibited or possessed unless the Board has approved it, and s.6 catches possession, not only exhibition. Festival and gallery screenings are inside it on the Board's own reading. The Appeal Committee is the first real remedy and filmmakers barely use it.

[documentary#02] FINAS Act 1981 (Act 244)  as-at 2013-08-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-244-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: FINAS licensing makes it an offence to carry on film production, distribution or exhibition without a licence. This is the provision that has been used against independent documentary makers and it is the most-criticised barrier to entry in Malaysian film.

[documentary#03] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: Archive footage, stills, music and the interview release you did not get. Clear it before the edit lock, not after.

[documentary#04] Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709)  as-at 2023-07-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-709-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: Contributors are data subjects. Consent, retention and the security of your rushes are all inside the Act, and the 2024 amendments added breach notification.

[documentary#05] Official Secrets Act 1972 (Act 88)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-88-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: A document classified as an official secret carries a mandatory custodial sentence and the Minister's certificate is conclusive as to classification, so there is no route to argue the material was innocuous.

[documentary#06] Defamation Act 1957 (Act 286)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-286-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: Justification, fair comment and qualified privilege are the defences that matter to investigative work, and Malaysia has no serious-harm threshold, so the bar to suing you is low.

[documentary#07] Immigration Act 1959/63 (Act 155)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-155-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: Foreign crew are working, not visiting.

[documentary#08] PUSPAL (guideline)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/puspal-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: Foreign filming needs PUSPAL clearance, and the conditions attached are administrative rather than statutory, so they change without any amendment being gazetted.

[documentary#09] Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (Act 514)  as-at 2024-06-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-514-bi.pdf
    ROLES: filmmaker
    CLAIM: A set is a workplace, and since 2022 the duty reaches the principal, not just the direct employer of the crew.

[documentary#10] Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 (Act 301)  as-at 2012-09-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-301-bi.pdf
    ROLES: exhibitor
    CLAIM: Programmes, catalogues and accompanying publications sit under a different regime again.

SCENARIO exhibit-sell-art  |  Exhibit and sell your work
LEDE: Selling the object is not selling the copyright, and the commission clause decides who owned it in the first place.
WATCH-CLAIM: Section 26(2) is the clause that quietly transfers ownership of commissioned work. Read it before you accept the commission, not after you see it reprinted.

[exhibit-sell-art#01] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, gallery
    CLAIM: Selling the physical work is not selling the copyright, and s.25 moral rights survive the sale. The trap is s.26(2): commissioned work and work made in the course of employment moves ownership unless you contract out of it, and almost nobody does.

[exhibit-sell-art#02] Contracts Act 1950 (Act 136)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-136-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, gallery
    CLAIM: Consignment, commission percentage, insurance while held, and what happens when the gallery closes owing you for sold work.

[exhibit-sell-art#03] Industrial Designs Act 1996 (Act 552)  as-at 2013-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-552-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: If the work is industrially applied, s.7(6) of the Copyright Act cuts your copyright and registration under this Act becomes the protection. Designers fall into this gap constantly.

[exhibit-sell-art#04] Consumer Protection Act 1999 (Act 599)  as-at 2021-11-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-599-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, gallery
    CLAIM: You are a seller. Implied guarantees, misleading conduct and the Tribunal for Consumer Claims all apply, and the Tribunal is cheap enough to be used against you.

[exhibit-sell-art#05] Stamp Act 1949 (Act 378)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-378-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, gallery
    CLAIM: An assignment or an exclusive representation agreement is an instrument.

[exhibit-sell-art#06] National Heritage Act 2005 (Act 645)  as-at 2006-06-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-645-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, gallery
    CLAIM: Heritage objects can be caught on export without anyone realising they were listed, and declaration constrains what an owner may do with their own property.

[exhibit-sell-art#07] Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709)  as-at 2023-07-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-709-bi.pdf
    ROLES: gallery
    CLAIM: Collector and mailing list records are personal data.

[exhibit-sell-art#08] Income Tax Act 1967 (Act 53)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-53-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: Whether your sales are taxable business income turns on the badges of trade, not on whether you call yourself a business.

SCENARIO mural  |  Paint a mural or install work in public space
LEDE: Your copyright survives on someone else's wall. What the owner may then do to it is the fight.
WATCH-CLAIM: Commissioners routinely assume that paying for a mural buys the copyright and the right to alter it. Section 26(2) may give them the first; s.25 does not give them the second.

[mural#01] Local Government Act 1976 (Act 171)  as-at 2012-12-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-171-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, commissioner
    CLAIM: Permission comes from the local authority under a by-law made under this Act, and the council can require removal. Get the permission in writing before the first coat.

[mural#02] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, commissioner
    CLAIM: Copyright in the mural is yours even though the wall is not. Section 25 moral rights, including integrity, are where the argument lands when a wall is painted over or altered.

[mural#03] National Heritage Act 2005 (Act 645)  as-at 2006-06-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-645-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, commissioner
    CLAIM: On or near a declared heritage site, what may be done to the fabric is controlled and the penalty falls on whoever did it.

[mural#04] Penal Code (Act 574)  as-at 2023-07-04  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-574-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: The obscenity provisions and s.298A on religious disharmony are the two that reach public imagery.

[mural#05] Minor Offences Act 1955 (Act 336)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-336-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: Public nuisance, read with the by-law, is the everyday enforcement route.

[mural#06] Contracts Act 1950 (Act 136)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-136-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist, commissioner
    CLAIM: The commission agreement should say who owns the copyright, whether it can be altered or removed, and who is liable if the council objects.

[mural#07] Federal Constitution (constitution)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/fedcon-bi.pdf
    ROLES: artist
    CLAIM: Article 10 when the objection is to the content rather than the paint.

SCENARIO publish-book  |  Publish a book or zine
LEDE: Printing needs a licence, publishing needs a permit, and the Minister can prohibit the result outright.
WATCH-CLAIM: Section 7 of the Printing Presses and Publications Act is the prohibition power and it has been used against books and graphic novels. Reviewability is the remedy people forget they now have.

[publish-book#01] Printing Presses and Publications Act 1984 (Act 301)  as-at 2012-09-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-301-bi.pdf
    ROLES: publisher, author
    CLAIM: Printing presses need a licence, publications need a permit, and the Minister may prohibit a publication outright. Zines, art books, catalogues, comics and independent presses are all inside this Act whether or not they think of themselves as publishers. The 2012 amendment removed the finality clause, so the Minister's decision is now judicially reviewable, and that is the opening.

[publish-book#02] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author, publisher
    CLAIM: Duration, first ownership, and the publishing agreement. Section 27 requires an exclusive licence to be in writing, and a publishing deal is usually an exclusive licence.

[publish-book#03] Defamation Act 1957 (Act 286)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-286-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author, publisher
    CLAIM: Memoir, reportage and anything naming a living person. Justification and fair comment are the defences; there is no serious-harm threshold here.

[publish-book#04] Sedition Act 1948 (Act 15)  as-at 2006-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-15-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author, publisher
    CLAIM: Seditious tendency, with intention expressly irrelevant under s.3(3). That is what makes it dangerous to satire.

[publish-book#05] Penal Code (Act 574)  as-at 2023-07-04  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-574-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author, publisher
    CLAIM: Sections 292 to 294 on obscene books and objects, and ss.298 and 298A on religious feelings.

[publish-book#06] Stamp Act 1949 (Act 378)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-378-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author, publisher
    CLAIM: The publishing agreement is an instrument.

[publish-book#07] Marrakesh Treaty (treaty)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/marrakesh-bi.pdf
    ROLES: publisher
    CLAIM: The basis for the accessible-format exceptions, which is the answer when a library or a reader asks.

[publish-book#08] Berne Convention (treaty)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/berne-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author
    CLAIM: Automatic protection in every member state, no formality required.

[publish-book#09] Income Tax Act 1967 (Act 53)  as-at 2026-01-01  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-53-bi.pdf
    ROLES: author
    CLAIM: Advances and royalties are income; the badges of trade decide the character.

SCENARIO stage-show  |  Stage a play, gig or live show
LEDE: Two sets of performing rights, a venue licence, a duty on admissions, and the question of what your cast actually are.
WATCH-CLAIM: The employment question is upstream of everything. Get it wrong and you have also got the safety duty, the harassment forum and the tax treatment wrong.

[stage-show#01] Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332)  as-at 2022-06-30  pdf: legislation/pdf/act-332-bi.pdf
    ROLES: producer
    CLAIM: The script and the music are separate permissions from separate owners, and a performing licence for one is not a licence for the other.

[stage-show#02] Collecting societies (collecting)  as-at n/a  pdf: legislation/pdf/cmos-bi.pdf
    ROLES: producer, venue
    CLAIM: Live and recorded music in the show engages the public performance rights administered by MACP, PRISM and RPM.
