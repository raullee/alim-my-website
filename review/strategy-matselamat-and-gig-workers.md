# MatSelamat, the Gig Workers Act, and what ALIM should actually do

Everything below is read off the hosted text of the Gig Workers Act 2025 and the Rules of
Court, not from memory. Section numbers are quotable.

---

## 1. The finding that changes the picture

**The Gig Workers Act 2025 is, in substantial part, an arts statute.** Its Schedule does
not describe platform work in the abstract. It names the work:

| | Service | Who it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acting | Actor |
| 2 | Filming activities | Film production crew |
| 3 | Music-related activities | Singer, musician, composer, lyricist |
| 4 | Aesthetic | Make-up artist, hair stylist, stylist |
| 5 | Translation | Sign interpreter, interpreter or translator, transcriber |
| 6 | Journalism | Journalist, stringer |
| 9 | Photography and videography | |

Seven of the nine listed services are creative and cultural work. This is the most
consequential piece of legislation for ALIM's constituency passed in years, and almost
nobody in the arts knows it applies to them, because it is filed in everyone's head under
"Grab riders".

---

## 2. How the claim routes actually compare

For an artist who has not been paid:

| | Gig Workers Tribunal | Small claims, O.93 |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Schedule-listed work, platform or not | anyone |
| Ceiling | **none stated.** s.33(1): any award "as it deems just and appropriate" | **RM5,000, hard stop** |
| Consequential loss | **yes**, s.33(3) | no |
| Advocates | **barred**, s.36(3) | not represented |
| Who may represent you | official of a **gig workers' association**, or a family member, s.36(2)(b) | nobody |
| Way in | conciliation first, s.18 to s.20; the Conciliator or Minister refers it up | file Form 198 yourself |
| Cost | free | filing fee |

Other routes, for completeness: Labour Department under s.69 Employment Act 1955 if you
were in truth an employee, with no cap on the amount but **Peninsular Malaysia only**;
s.20 Industrial Relations Act for dismissal, 60 days; Tribunal for Consumer Claims, which
in practice is used *against* an artist who sold something, not by one.

**So the honest answer to "where does a musician owed RM8,000 go" is not small claims.**
Small claims cannot even hear it. The Gig Workers Tribunal can, for free, with consequential
loss on top.

---

## 3. What that does to MatSelamat

MatSelamat is a Form 198 generator for small claims, aimed at performing artists. Read the
two columns above again: **statute has just built a better route for exactly that user.**

That is not fatal, but it does mean the product as currently framed is aimed at a shrinking
niche. Three honest observations.

1. **The form is no longer the hard part. The choice of forum is.** Pick wrong and you lose
   months, or you cap yourself at RM5,000 when you did not have to, or you file in court
   when conciliation was free. The valuable thing MatSelamat can do is not generate Form 198,
   it is ask six questions and tell you which of five routes you are in, then produce the
   right document for that route.
2. **The general public is the durable market, not artists.** Anyone in Malaysia owed under
   RM5,000 still needs small claims, because the Schedule does not cover most people. That
   market is bigger than the arts and is not being eaten by Act 872.
3. **The arts market is now a routing market.** For a Schedule-listed artist, MatSelamat's
   job is to get them to conciliation, not to court.

---

## 4. The opening nobody has seen

**Section 36(3) bars advocates and solicitors from the Gig Workers Tribunal.
Section 36(2)(b) permits an official of a gig workers' association to represent the worker.**

A "gig workers' association" is defined in s.2 as an association **registered under the
Societies Act 1966** consisting of gig workers, for the purpose of representing their
interests and welfare. Section 10 makes void any term of a service agreement that restricts
a gig worker's right to form, join or take part in one.

Two consequences, and they are both large.

**First, this is a lawful representation route for non-lawyers.** It is not a workaround; the
statute names who may appear and expressly excludes advocates. That is the opposite of the
problem that has been blocking Setara, where the constraint is s.37(3) of the Legal
Profession Act. Here the legislature has decided the question the other way.

**Second, ALIM as currently planned cannot use it.** The board chose a company limited by
guarantee under the Companies Act 2016. A gig workers' association must be registered under
the Societies Act 1966. **A CLBG cannot be one.**

So there is a structural decision on the table that nobody has framed yet:

- keep the CLBG, and separately help artists constitute a **registered society** of
  Schedule-listed creative workers, which ALIM supports, trains and staffs but does not
  control; or
- reconsider the vehicle; or
- do nothing, and accept that ALIM can advise on the Tribunal but cannot appear in it.

Before any of that, two things need checking that I have not verified: whether an official
of the association must themselves be a gig worker, and whether the Registrar of Societies
will register an association of this kind without friction. Both are answerable in an
afternoon and both are prior to the decision.

This is the single most valuable thing to bring to the next meeting, and it has nothing to
do with hosting costs.

## 5. What the integrated thing should look like

**Do not merge the brands. Split by audience and keep both.**

| | ALIM instance | MatSelamat |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Schedule-listed creative workers | anyone in Malaysia |
| Job | which of five routes am I in, then the paperwork | small claims, done properly |
| Brand | ALIM's own | Axon's |
| Owner | ALIM | Axon |
| Cost to ALIM | nil | n/a |

Reasons to keep them separate rather than absorbing one into the other:

- ALIM builds its own brand equity instead of building a director's.
- MatSelamat keeps the general market, which is where its future is and which ALIM has no
  business chasing.
- The conflict stays small and declared instead of becoming structural.
- If ALIM ever needs to walk away from it, there is nothing to unpick.

**On the name.** Two things worth raising with the Malay speakers on the board rather than
deciding alone. "Selamat" means safe, and the product is now about getting paid, so the name
is slightly off its own message. And "Mat Selamat" sits one letter from "Mas Selamat", which
carries a well-known regional news association. Neither is fatal and I am not asserting
either is a problem; both are cheap to check and expensive to discover later.

For the ALIM instance, the site already has `/small-claim-drafter/` published as a Small
Claims Guide. The natural move is to widen it from one route to five and name it for what
the user wants rather than for the procedure. Something in the register of the rest of the
site, on the pattern of "Get paid".

## 6. The game theory, stated plainly

**What is scarce here is not money. It is standing, and a clean organisation when the grant
money arrives.**

Set out what each side is actually worth:

| Asset | Value to Raul | Value to ALIM |
|---|---|---|
| MatSelamat | dormant, no revenue, no distribution outside the arts | a working tool it would otherwise build or go without |
| AI subscription, ~RM5,400/yr | would be paid anyway, for RLB and Axon | the largest line in its budget, paid to a director |
| Hosting, ~RM400/yr | real out-of-pocket | a line it already had and cancelled |
| alim.my, the library, the tooling | little outside ALIM; the harvester is reusable | its single most distinctive asset |
| ALIM itself | access to arts and culture funders Axon cannot reach; standing; a live reference for the CPD and s.90A work | |

Read that table and the optimal play is not subtle. **Trade what is abundant for what is
scarce.**

**Do**

1. **Settle the intellectual property before incorporation.** Nobody has raised this and it
   is the highest-stakes item on the list. Right now it is genuinely unclear who owns
   alim.my, the 87 library pages, the 19 situation guides and the harvester. If ALIM wins a
   grant on the strength of the library and the position is unresolved, that is a problem
   for ALIM and for you. The clean split: **assign the output to ALIM** (the site, the
   content, the library) and **licence the machinery** to it non-exclusively while keeping
   it, because the harvester is reusable across your other projects and has value outside
   ALIM. ALIM owns what it shows the world; you keep the engine.
2. **Give the arts instance of MatSelamat free, and keep the general brand.** Costs nothing,
   ends the conflict question, preserves the commercial option.
3. **Move hosting and the domain onto ALIM's own account.** Not a reimbursement to you, an
   account in its name. Restores the RM400 line it already had. Takes the domain off RLB's
   billing, which should not be where it sits.
4. **Bring the s.36(2)(b) finding to the board as the headline.** Walk in with the strategic
   opening, not with the invoice. The person who found ALIM's route to lawful representation
   is not the person anyone suspects of working an angle.

**Do not**

5. **Do not ask the charity to fund the subscription.** It would be the largest line in the
   budget, paid to a director, for a tool you would buy anyway, in an organisation whose
   constitution will very likely bar applying funds for a director's benefit. It is the one
   move that risks the thing that is actually valuable, to recover a sum that is not.
6. **Do not take equity-like positions in a CLBG.** There are none to take. Nobody owns a
   company limited by guarantee. Your return is the funder access, the standing and the
   commercial arm, not the entity.

**Where your economics actually live**

The charitable side stays clean and you contribute to it. **The commercial arm is where you
get paid**: courses, merch, managed cases, and tooling. That is the structure that gets you
to "not out of pocket" overall without leaning on the charity for a single ringgit beyond
what it already spent. If the association route in section 4 works, the commercial arm also
has an obvious service line attached to it.

Raise the commercial arm at the same meeting, as the answer to funding, and the cost
conversation stops being about you.
